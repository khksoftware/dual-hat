# Publication and Closure

Engineering completion enters Architecture Review. Only an Architecture-owned `accepted` or non-blocking `accepted_with_follow_up` disposition authorizes deterministic archival; remediation remains active. Engineering may recommend but cannot self-accept or archive.

Branch and push behavior comes from the active work order and product profile. Use forward-only correction: do not rewrite published history, force-push, or overwrite unexplained external work. Before publication, verify branch, remotes, history, cleanliness, prior governed marker, target drift, credentials, and authorization.

**A push publishes its whole ancestry, so verify the ancestry rather than the ref.** Naming an explicit ref protects only against publishing commits *ahead* of yours; commits another writer landed *behind* yours on the same branch are ancestors by the time you push, and the push output reports the ref you named rather than the effect it had. So before every push to a shared branch, enumerate the commits between the remote tip and the commit being pushed, and read them. **Every commit that enumeration names is one this session created, or the push does not happen.** A commit another writer has made and not yet published is that writer's to publish: it may be held back deliberately — on its own verification, its own sequencing, its own publication gate — and nothing visible from outside distinguishes a deliberate hold from unfinished work. Where the two are indistinguishable, do not publish on another writer's behalf. This is a separate gate from the validation verdict and both bind the same push: a green candidate does not authorize publishing a foreign commit, and a clean ancestry does not authorize publishing an unvalidated one.

Where history is forward-only, resequencing is not an available remedy. Wait for the owning writer to publish its own commit; where waiting genuinely blocks — a shared surface this writer must publish is stuck behind the foreign commit, or the owning writer has gone quiet — escalate the sequencing rather than resolving it by pushing. **Nothing enforces this wherever every writer commits under one identity**: authorship then carries no signal a pre-push check could refuse on, and the only party that reliably knows which commits are its own is the session that made them, which is exactly where such a check cannot look. An adopter whose writers carry distinguishable identities may arm it; one whose writers do not holds it by reading.

When a profile uses short-lived work branches, closure integrates the accepted work into its authorized target and retires or repurposes the completed branch before unrelated work continues. Retaining a completed branch requires an explicit owner, purpose, and retirement trigger. A new work item does not silently continue on the prior item's branch.

Publication compares the candidate with the prior owned file set, reports adds/changes/removals/renames, applies only governed changes, commits transparently, pushes when authorized, fetches, and verifies HEAD/upstream alignment plus a clean worktree. A failed push leaves the local commit intact and is reported as blocked, never as success.

Closure inventory covers every created, modified, moved, split, generated, exported, archived, deleted, and temporary artifact. Reproducible staging, caches, worktrees, process logs, and export debris are removed. Rollback states which commit, migration reversal, external action, or restored marker is safe; recovery never assumes chat history.

Active and output locations contain only current operationally consumed artifacts. At capability or phase closure, and whenever an artifact is superseded, classify capability- or phase-scoped outputs as still current, historical evidence, or disposable duplication. Retain current artifacts in place, move historical evidence to the designated archive with sufficient hash/path traceability, and remove disposable copies. Historical usefulness alone does not justify leaving evidence on a current product surface. A terminally archived work item's own active tracking or working location moves to the archive as a whole in this same closure step; classifying its individual outputs does not substitute for relocating the location itself.

Closure also reconciles the approved work order and conversation record for explicitly promised stakeholder-facing findings, comparisons, recommendations, reports, and decision summaries. A promised delivery is complete only when it has been proactively presented to the stakeholder, or explicitly reported as deferred or blocked; creating, committing, or archiving an artifact is not delivery. Use one compact exit-report checklist rather than a new ledger, and do not wait for the stakeholder to remember or request the promised result.

Every capability's closing gate includes a mandatory independent reconciliation audit. The reviewer is context-isolated from the work under review — the same cold-context reviewer pattern already used elsewhere in this framework — and is never the same agent or context that executed the work. The audit reconciles three sources against verified repository fact rather than self-report — facts, not words: the sealed work order's approved scope; every incremental request or instruction the stakeholder gave during execution, which may have expanded, corrected, or redirected scope beyond the original seal; and every interim finding, bug, or gap identified during execution that was committed to being addressed. For each reconciled item the audit states a disposition of done, partially done, or not done, supported by cited evidence — a commit hash, file path, or test name and result — never a narrative completion claim. A disposition of partially done or not done blocks closure until resolved or explicitly deferred by the author. This rule is product-neutral and applies to every capability regardless of product or profile.
