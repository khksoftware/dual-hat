# Dual Hat 1.15.0 Release Notes

Dual Hat 1.15.0 strengthens independent judgment, truthful scope reporting,
systemic learning, and research completeness without removing or weakening any
existing 1.x contract.

The independent specialist roster now explicitly includes
Architecture/Design, UX, and QA. Projects invoke only the specialists whose
risks are material, and each reviewer seeks disconfirming evidence and relevant
failure paths without turning ordinary low-risk work into three mandatory
gates.

Long-running work now has a one-to-five-minute verified progress cadence.
Delegation does not transfer communication accountability, and opaque workers
receive tighter monitoring. Completion claims must name their exact inventory,
reconcile counts and dispositions, and state what remains; finishing a sample
or wave cannot silently become completion of its parent objective.

When a stakeholder or another agent identifies a real error, the responsible
role runs a correction-to-control loop: repair the instance, generalize the
failure mode, identify its owning cause, apply and codify the smallest
effective reusable countermeasure, inspect direct analogues, and report the
disposition proportionately.

Material testable hypotheses may use preregistered hypothesis-blind execution
and a separate blinded result reviewer. When a material decision remains
genuinely doubtful, exactly three isolated arbiters research it independently;
a valid `3:0` or `2:1` vote decides only within authority already delegated to
the framework.

Representative samples no longer justify source- or population-wide exclusion
unless a preregistered sampling design supports that inference. Enumerable
corpora intended for broad intake require item inventory first and separate
catalog, triage, and processing completeness. Before Architecture or
Engineering narrows external-source discovery or ingestion, a sealed
independent reviewer must approve or reject the restriction.

This is a backward-compatible additive governance release. Existing 1.x
authority, lifecycle, schema, profile, handover, and compatibility contracts
remain unchanged.
